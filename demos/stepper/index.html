Stepping OCaml, in the browser

Kenichi Asai's stepper (Ochanomizu), compiled with js_of_ocaml and running entirely in this page. Pick an example from the notes or write your own, then step through it. With predict on, you say what the next line is before you see it. The yellow part is what reduces next, and the green part is what it became.

The last let is the expression that's stepped. Programs must be closed and well typed. The standard library's List isn't available.

    Known limits of the stepper
    • Arguments are evaluated right to left, as OCaml does. So in preorder the right subtree is visited first (see that example).
    • while, for, mutable fields and lazy aren't supported, and a function with a when guard is shown wrongly.
    • It adds more parentheses than the notes use. The predict check ignores spaces and parentheses when it compares your line.
